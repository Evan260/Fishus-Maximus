# Fishus Maximus: Claude Code Guide

A cozy Roman fishing game in Godot 4.7 (GDScript, 2D, GL Compatibility), set on the Bay of Naples in AD 72. The design is in `Overview.md`, the detailed rules in `Mechanics.md`, and the build plan in `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-06-full-game.md`.

## Where things are

- `game/scripts/autoload/`: `Dev` (command-line options and screenshots), `Data` (everything in `game/data/*.json`), `Settings` (volumes, the window, gentle fishing, text size), `Game` (the game being played, one dictionary; keys starting with `_` and a fight in progress are never saved; `catch_up()` lets time away pass; `demo_state()` is a game some way in), `Saves` (three slots, each with a `.bak`, saved every minute and on every change of screen), `Nav` (screens and the world and interface layers), `Windowing` (the desktop strip, F10), `Audio` (effects by file name, ambience loops, music).
- `game/scripts/rules/`: the rules as plain classes, tested headless. `World.advance` is the one clock: pots, freshness, helpers, and each new morning the market, orders, ponds, tanks, weather and the pond show. Also `Clock`, `Catalog`, `Bite`, `LineFight`, `Catches`, `Hold`, `Traps`, `Nets`, `Trident`, `Idle`, `Market`, `Orders`, `Story` (chapters and clients), `Shop`, `Ponds`, `Garum`, `Weather`, `Events`, `Legends`, `Achievements`, `Helpers`, `Gossip`, `Dogs`, and `Guide` (old Rufus's lessons for a new player, in `data/guide.json`: the first not yet learned is shown, each cleared by a count in `stats` or the logbook, and a lesson once learned stays learned).
- `game/scripts/coast/`: a fishing place seen side on. `coast_screen.gd` holds the line, the bite, the strike and the fight; `ParallaxLayers` slides each painted layer at its own speed, `FloorView` lays the painted sand strip along the seabed line, `WaterView` draws the water by depth, `BoatView` the hull and the fisherman from `assets/art/fisherman/`: seated with his rod, turned to face the stern and rowing on a boat with `oars` in `boats.json`, winding back and throwing to cast, with the oar and the rod drawn in his hands (a boat whose sail or high sides hide the seat names `seat` and `dog` in `boats.json`: how far along, and the row), `DogView` the dog (drawn between the boat and the hull's near side so it sits down inside it; sitting and lying down play once and hold), `Swimmers` the fish (a fish keeps to water at least `Catalog.water_needed()` deep, more for bigger fish, from `fishing.deep_water` in `balance.json`; each wanders in curves, changes pace, pauses and eases round to turn, its tail waved by `Pixels.look`; `wave`, `waves` and `leaps` in `fish.json` set a kind apart), `LineView` the line, the float and bars, the hooked fish and its leaps clear of the water, `CoastMethods` pots, net, trident and fishing on his own (a fish he catches is kept at once, then shown taking the float under and coming in on the line before it is told of), `CoastEvents` dolphins, the tuna haul and the Neptunalia, `SkyView` the sky by the hour in `Nav.sky` (Farmus's banded gradient, sun, moon and stars, and its clouds from `assets/art/sky/` in three depths, more and greyer when it is overcast; `SkyPalette` holds the colours), `CoastCamera` the camera (it rises while he rows so the view looks over the water, rises with a flying float, and keeps the float on screen).
- A place in `game/data/places.json` lists its `seabed` line, its `floor` strip, `ground_below` (below the seabed plus this the ground is solid shadow), its `map` pin and its layers: `file` (under `assets/art`), `at` (on screen with the camera at home, the waterline 160 pixels down) or `world` (world pixels, the waterline at 0), `factor`, `z`, `mirror`, `alpha`, `repeat`, `through` (crosses the waterline, so its lower part is tinted), or `band` (the sea under a row of land, shading into the band behind it). Each band of sea is drawn by `sea.gdshader`, mirroring the sky's colours for the hour and weather from `SkyView.look()`, with the sun's or moon's path on it; each land picture over a band draws its own reflection with `reflection.gdshader`, starting at its waterline per column (`ParallaxLayers.waterlines()`), darker and tinted toward the band, broken by ripples that grow in rain. A `front` layer (`"top"` or `"bottom"`) is a dark plant right in front of the view from `assets/art/front/`, hanging from the view's top edge or standing on its bottom one: `x` is the place along the coast it stands in front of and `factor` (above 1) how much faster than the boat it slides. It breaks away in blocks of two pixels (`front.gdshader`, Farmus's market dissolve) while the boat, the float, a fish fight or a button along the bottom of the screen is behind it (`ParallaxLayers.keep_clear()`), and comes back the same way; a branch rises out of the view as it dips into the water, a plant at the bottom breaks away then, and none shows in the desktop strip. A layer with `sway` (seagrass, kelp, reeds and the like) sways by whole texels in `sway.gdshader`. Pieces at z -9 stand behind the floor; the floor hides their bases.
- `game/scripts/town/`, `shore/`, `map/`, `screens/`: the town street and its panels, the ponds and garum works, the bay map, and the title.
- `game/scripts/ui/`: `UiTheme` (pixel fonts and the painted panels and buttons in `assets/art/ui/`), `Ui` (helpers; money is always `Ui.coins_text`; `Ui.window` for panels; `Ui.tile` and `Ui.price_tile` are picture buttons with a tip, which the shops, the tackle and the boat's bar are made of), `ItemArt` (the picture of a bait, gear, help, a way of fishing, a boat or a fish), `Hud` (the ways of fishing as pictures along the bottom, and beside the line the bait and the rod, which open the tackle), `Toast`, `CatchCard`, `HoldChoice`, `LogbookPanel`, `SettingsPanel`, `AchievementCard`, `TacklePanel` (the bait on the hook and the rod in hand, each with a tip naming the fish here it suits; a rod suits the fish sizes in its `best` range in `gear.json`, and `Bite.rod_fit` makes smaller fish shy of it), `GuideNote` (old Rufus in the top right corner with the lesson for the coast or the town, an arrow at what to click from the screen's `guide_target(id)`, Got it and Skip the guide; the settings turn him back on), `Controls` (keys and controller), `Pixels` (lines, rings, discs and polygons drawn as whole game pixels, and `look()`, the shader that keeps a scaled or turned picture's pixels whole).
- `game/tests/`: `run_tests.gd` runs every `test_*` method in every `test_*.gd`; a test that raises an engine error fails. `pacing_bot.gd` and `flow_bot.gd` are the bots.
- `game/tools/style_tests/`: the two approved style tests, kept as references and never exported.
- `tools/art/`: `pixellab.py` (the token is in `.env`, never in code; every picture's cost goes in `ledger.csv`), `make_places.py` with the specs written by `places/_write_specs.py` (land with a `style` picture is painted to match it, as the harbour's cape and the rocky shore are to `places/style_land.png`, a piece of the harbour town, and the harbour's hills and mountains, Lucrine's shores and Capri's cliffs are to the cape itself; a `tone` then brings every row of land into the cape's colours with `tone_land.py`, its greens to the cape's olive scrub and dark pines and its rock to the cape's limestone, with a little haze the farther back it lies, so all the land shares one look), `make_front.py` (the dark plants in front of the view, painted in the cape's style, cut to run off the edge they stand on and darkened to near silhouettes; Roman plants only, so a dwarf fan palm rather than an agave), `make_harbour_town.py` (the harbour's row of town, its lighthouse shortened rather than cut so the row still slopes to the sea), `make_fish.py`, `make_props.py` (boats, pots, net, lamp, dolphin, jetty; the rowing boats are painted to match a style picture so no oar comes back, and the jetty to match the harbour town), `round_keels.py` (rounds each boat's flat bottom into a keel curving up to bow and stern), `make_items.py` (the pictures in `assets/art/items/` of everything for sale, the help for hire and the ways of fishing), `make_fisherman.py` (the fisherman's seated pose, rowing stroke and cast from his PixelLab character, with where his hands are in every frame), `soften_outlines.py` (turns a sprite's near-black outline into a darker shade of the colour inside it; run it on anything new), `clear_sky.py` (takes painted clouds and sky out of a row of land so the game's drifting sky shows there), `mute_colours.py` (tones down an over-bright sprite), `make_icon.py` (the exe and taskbar icon from a scene painted in the title's style), `make_town.py` (the street, its buildings painted into it, then any wall or paving painted inside a building's outline given back to the street so each ends at its own edges), `make_portraits.py` (the clients' faces for their visits, each painted to match the person's own figure in the street), `make_people.py` (the townsfolk and clients in the street, each a PixelLab character in the Farmus dog's style with an eight-frame walk; check each by eye for dark outlines), `make_shore.py`, `make_title.py` (the title from the sunset style test: the cliff carried on under the road, the sky cleared of clouds, and the whole painted clouds plus three painted to match cut out to drift), `make_seabed.py` (each place's sand strip drawn without PixelLab in its spec's `floor.sand` colour: smooth sand with faint speckle and a lighter top edge, joining onto itself and darkening into the ground; `make_places.py` draws a missing floor with it), `make_jetty.py` (the harbour jetty with its posts carried down past the sea floor, which hides their feet), `check_edges.py` (fails any layer whose edge could show as a straight cut; `tiles.txt` lists the exceptions), and `places_json.py`.
- `tools/audio/cut_sounds.py` cuts the sea sounds from the CC0 recordings in `tools/audio/recordings/`.
- `tools/art/shore.py` gives a row of land its shore where it meets the sea (beaches, rocks in the water, bushes, reeds and small coves, coloured from the picture itself) and fades sea painted along its bottom into the band; `make_places.py` runs it once for each land layer with a `shore` in its spec, keeping the picture as it was in `parts/<place>/<name>_inland.png`, so deleting `<name>.shore` shores it again.

## Fixed decisions

- The game is laid out at 480 by 270 game pixels, each the largest whole number of window pixels that fits, as Farmus does (Godot's own stretch is off: its stretch modes show a maximised window at the size it opened at). `Nav.port` draws it at the window's resolution (`size_2d_override` with its stretch, and every corner snapped to a window pixel), so moving things slide by fractions of a game pixel while each texel stays whole. Nothing that moves is rounded to a game pixel; a line or shape is drawn with `Pixels`, and a picture scaled or turned with `Pixels.look()`. `Nav.sky` draws behind the game at the window's resolution too. It opens windowed; F11 switches to full screen; F10 the desktop strip.
- Every balance number lives in `game/data/*.json`, read with `Data.num("time.day_minutes")`. The chapters open at the names in `chapters.json`, tuned with the pacing bot.
- Art is painted with PixelLab without outlines, and no picture's edge may ever show as a straight cut: land carries on below the water and rock runs to the bottom of the world.
- A floor, wall or quay is one painted piece, joined onto itself when it repeats; pieces painted apart are never set side by side, and an old picture never gets new sides painted on. Check every scene by eye at game scale, since the edge script cannot see a change of texture.
- A texture is loaded before drawing starts; one first loaded inside a draw call shows as a blank square.

## Running and checking

- Godot: `/c/Users/evand/AppData/Local/Microsoft/WinGet/Packages/GodotEngine.GodotEngine_Microsoft.Winget.Source_8wekyb3d8bbwe/Godot_v4.7.2-stable_win64_console.exe`
- After adding a class or any picture or sound: `--headless --path game --import`.
- Tests: `--headless --path game -- --run-tests` must print `ALL TESTS PASSED`; `--only=NAME` runs the test files whose names contain NAME.
- Any screen alone: `--path game -- --screen=NAME --no-save --shot-at=2 --shot=OUT.png --quit`; `--demo-state` starts a game some way in (`--boat=ID` swaps its boat); `--guide=ID` starts a new game at that lesson of the guide; `--click=X,Y` clicks at window pixels at `--click-at` seconds.
- A place: `--screen=coast --place=ID`, with `--hour=H`, `--weather=rain`, `--at=X` (the boat) or `--at=X,Y` (the boat, and the camera held at that depth), `--demo-cast` (casts, strikes and reels by itself; `--hook=ID` has that fish take the bait at once, and `--leap` has it leap whenever it may), `--idle` (fishes on his own from the start, `--idle-speed=N` times as fast), `--row=1` or `--row=-1` (rows that way for `--row-for` seconds), `--tackle` (opens the tackle) and `--strip`, with `--solid` (not see-through), `--strip-width=N`, `--strip-scale=N` and `--strip-controls` (keeps the right-hand controls showing).
- The town with a shop open: `--screen=town --shop=market` (or `netmaker`, `boatwright`, `goldsmith`).
- Every screen in one go: `--path game -- --flow=DIR --no-save` saves a screenshot of each.
- Pacing: `--headless --path game -- --pacing --no-save` prints when each chapter, boat and legend arrives for three keen players over 40 hours. Targets: chapter 2 in 1 to 2 hours, 3 in 4 to 6, 4 in 9 to 13, 5 in 16 to 22, every legend within 40.
- After painting a place: `python tools/art/check_edges.py` must print `no visible cut edges`.
- Build: `--headless --path game --export-release "Windows" "../builds/windows/Fishus Maximus.exe"`, with the trimmed engine copied from Mosaicus Maximus (`tools/engine/`, git-ignored; no 3D, physics, navigation, regex or noise). Copy `CREDITS.md` beside it as `Credits and licences.txt`.
