# Fishus Maximus: Claude Code Guide

A cozy Roman fishing game in Godot 4.7 (GDScript, 2D, GL Compatibility), set on the Bay of Naples in AD 72. The design is in `Overview.md`, the detailed rules in `Mechanics.md`, and the build plan in `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-06-full-game.md`.

## Where things are

- `game/scripts/autoload/`: `Dev` (command-line options and screenshots), `Data` (everything in `game/data/*.json`), `Settings` (volumes for everything, music, effects and the ambience of sea, gulls and rain; the window, the interface size, kilograms or pounds (`units`, kilograms by default), gentle fishing, text size), `Game` (the game being played, one dictionary; keys starting with `_` and a fight in progress are never saved; `catch_up()` lets time away pass; `demo_state()` is a game some way in), `Saves` (three slots, each with a `.bak`, saved every minute and on every change of screen), `Nav` (screens and the world and interface layers; `cover()` lays a screen's picture over the whole window at whole window pixels per picture pixel, keeping its 480 by 270 scene in view when the picture is painted a `margin` past it, so no mirrored copy shows), `Windowing` (the desktop strip, F10), `Audio` (effects by file name, ambience loops, music).
- `game/scripts/rules/`: the rules as plain classes, tested headless. `World.advance` is the one clock: pots, freshness, helpers, and each new morning the market, orders, ponds, tanks, weather and the pond show. Also `Clock`, `Catalog`, `Bite`, `LineFight`, `Catches`, `Hold`, `Traps`, `Nets`, `Trident`, `Idle`, `Market`, `Orders`, `Story` (chapters and clients), `Shop`, `Ponds`, `Garum`, `Weather`, `Events`, `Legends`, `Achievements`, `Helpers`, `Gossip`, `Dogs`, `Rescue` (old Rufus's worms for a player with no bait, no money for any and nothing to sell, as Uncle Marcus helps in Farmus; `start.rescue_worms`), and `Guide` (old Rufus's lessons for a new player, in `data/guide.json`: the first not yet learned is shown, each cleared by a count in `stats` or the logbook, and a lesson once learned stays learned).
- `game/scripts/coast/`: a fishing place seen side on. `coast_screen.gd` holds the line, the bite, the strike and the fight; `ParallaxLayers` slides each painted layer at its own speed, `FloorView` lays the sand strip along the seabed line, `WaterView` draws the sea by depth, `UnderLand` the land carried on under the water as two darker shades of it (the far slope under the nearest row of land, reaching the surface under its tall hills and falling away between them, and a nearer bank of mounds behind the seabed), `Murk` the murky water over everything under the waterline (drawn after the fish, it reads back the screen, mutes it a little toward a sea grey and lays the water's colour over it more with depth, so the water stays a sea; plants, pieces on the seabed and fish first lose most of their own colour in their shaders, by the shares in `Murk`, and the lamp's light, the line and the boat are drawn after it), `BoatView` the hull and the fisherman from `assets/art/fisherman/`: seated with his rod, turned to face the stern and rowing on a boat with `oars` in `boats.json`, winding back and throwing to cast, with the oar and the rod drawn in his hands (a boat whose sail or high sides hide the seat names `seat` and `dog` in `boats.json`: how far along, and the row; `seat`'s row is where the near rail starts, so the far rail is drawn behind him and the dog), `DogView` the dog (drawn between the boat and the hull's near side so it sits down inside it, and cut off a little below the near rail so it never shows under the keel; sitting and lying down play once and hold), `Swimmers` the fish (each drawn one texel to a pixel, never scaled, so no rows double; after a place opens, new fish start out of sight past the view's sides and swim in; a biting fish puts its mouth, the front column of its picture (`mouth_of`), on the middle of the bait and follows the hook as the float drifts; a fish keeps to water at least `Catalog.water_needed()` deep, more for bigger fish, from `fishing.deep_water` in `balance.json`; a fish of a shoal takes a bait only `fishing.shoal_bite` as often as one alone, so the little shoal fish bite now and then without crowding out the rest; they spawn spread out along the water near the camera, `APART` from each other and fewer in the shallows (`room_for_fish`), each put where it swims at once; each wanders in curves, changes pace, pauses and eases round to turn, its tail waved by `Pixels.look`; `wave`, `waves` and `leaps` in `fish.json` set a kind apart), `LineView` the line, the float and bars, each bait drawn on the hook as a few pixels of its own (`BAITS`), the hooked fish and its leaps clear of the water, `CoastMethods` pots, net, trident and fishing on his own (a fish he catches is kept at once, then shown taking the float under and coming in on the line before it is told of), `CoastEvents` dolphins, the tuna haul and the Neptunalia, `SkyView` the sky by the hour in `Nav.sky` (Farmus's banded gradient, sun, moon and stars, and its clouds from `assets/art/sky/` in three depths, more and greyer when it is overcast; Farmus's northern lights on rare clear nights (`aurora.gdshader`, `sky.aurora_chance` in `balance.json`, `--aurora` or `--aurora=rising`); flocks of gulls by day and swallows at dusk; `SkyPalette` holds the colours), `CoastCamera` the camera (it rises while he rows so the view looks over the water, rises with a flying float, and keeps the float on screen).
- A place in `game/data/places.json` lists its `seabed` line, its `floor` strip, `ground_below` (below the seabed plus this the ground is solid shadow), its `map` pin and its layers: `file` (under `assets/art`), `at` (on screen with the camera at home, the waterline 160 pixels down) or `world` (world pixels, the waterline at 0), `factor`, `z`, `mirror`, `alpha`, `repeat`, `through` (crosses the waterline, so its lower part is tinted), or `band` (the sea under a row of land, shading into the band behind it). Each band of sea is drawn by `sea.gdshader`, mirroring the sky's colours for the hour and weather from `SkyView.look()`, with the sun's or moon's path on it; each land picture over a band draws its own reflection with `reflection.gdshader`, starting at its waterline per column (`ParallaxLayers.waterlines()`), darker and tinted toward the band, broken by ripples that grow in rain. A `front` layer (`"top"` or `"bottom"`) is a dark plant right in front of the view from `assets/art/front/`, hanging from the view's top edge or standing on its bottom one: `x` is the place along the coast it stands in front of and `factor` (above 1) how much faster than the boat it slides. It breaks away in blocks of two pixels (`front.gdshader`, Farmus's market dissolve) while the boat, the float, a fish fight or a button along the bottom of the screen is behind it (`ParallaxLayers.keep_clear()`), and comes back the same way; a branch rises out of the view as it dips into the water, a plant at the bottom breaks away then, and none shows in the desktop strip. A layer with `sway` (seagrass, kelp, reeds and the like) sways by whole texels in `sway.gdshader`. Pieces at z -9 stand behind the floor; the floor hides their bases. A piece set in the water with `world` is drawn dim and with little of its own colour (`Murk.shade_for`, `bed.gdshader`, and `sway.gdshader` for plants).
- `game/scripts/town/`, `shore/`, `map/`, `screens/`: the town street and its panels, the ponds and garum works, the bay map, and the title (`title.gd`: the harbour at sunset built from the coast's own pieces, its sky, layers, plants in front, water, land under the water, floor, murk and fish, with the camera held still and centred in a view of any shape, so it always looks as the game does; it runs before a game is loaded, so it reads nothing from `Game.state` and its `Swimmers` have `with_legends` off). The town and the ponds each lay one picture painted past their 480 by 270 scene (`street.png`, `shore.png`) over the window with `Nav.cover(margin)`, the margin read from the picture's size and the picture drawn at minus the margin so the scene keeps its own coordinates; no mirrored copies. Both show the coast's live sky through their cleared sky with `AshoreSky` (a `SkyView` in `Nav.sky` by the hour and the weather, its horizon kept on the screen's `HORIZON` row), and light the picture and the people by `SkyView.light(hour)`, dimmed in rain, while the gold outline and the names over them stay bright. The street is a covered hall: its beam rests on the hall's wooden post at the left and the stone pillar at the right, the fish market, the netmaker, the boatwright and the goldsmith stand as stalls along the wall between them, and the harbour board stands at the far left on the paving in front of the left post, turned to face the street; the townsfolk turn back before it (`WALK`). The town's lamps at `LAMPS` (the lantern in the goldsmith's stall, the hall's lantern on its left post and the oil lamp under the fish market's awning) are each a `Lamp`, as Farmus's are: a `PointLight2D` with a soft round texture that lights the street and the people round it, and an additive halo over the flame, outside the lit layer so the night does not dim it. They burn faintly by day and brightest at night or in rain, each flickering on its own; the outline and names keep `light_mask` 0, so the lamplight leaves them as they are. A building in the town is found by the mouse on its cut-out's opaque pixels, and its gold outline is every pixel touching those on a side or a corner, past the picture's edges too, so it runs unbroken all the way round.
- `game/scripts/ui/`: `UiTheme` (pixel fonts and the painted panels and buttons in `assets/art/ui/`), `Ui` (helpers; money is always `Ui.coins_text`; `Ui.window` for panels; `Ui.tile` and `Ui.price_tile` are picture buttons with a tip, which the shops, the tackle and the boat's bar are made of; `Ui.speech` is Farmus's speech bubble over someone's head, which the townsfolk talk in), `Units` (every weight is kept in pounds and shown through `Units.weight`, or `Units.need` for a weight asked for or suited, rounded as a person would say it, in kilograms or pounds by the settings; a client's words in `clients.json` write `{weight}` for the weight asked for, filled by `Units.fill`), `ItemArt` (the picture of a bait, gear, help, a way of fishing, a boat or a fish), `Hud` (the ways of fishing as pictures along the bottom, after the bay map's picture that the coast hangs in the corner; clicking the line opens the tackle just above it; the logbook, settings and menu are pictures in the top right corner), `Toast`, `CatchCard` (the banner hung out when a fish is landed, a painted vexillum in parts from `assets/art/ui/banner_*.png`: a gilded eagle over a wooden crossbar with gold tassels, a crimson cloth fringed in gold carrying the fish's name in gilt letters (`gilt.gdshader`), the fish on a marble tablet with handles at the most whole pixels per pixel that fit, as wide as the face and standing over the frame above and below it, its weight and grade, and a golden laurel wreath round the eagle's ball when it is new to the logbook or a legend; it drops in, unfurls from a rolled cloth, hangs for `SHOWN` seconds with its tassels and fringe swaying by whole texels (`banner_sway.gdshader`) and rolls up again; in the desktop strip the catch is one line of `Toast` instead), `HoldChoice`, `LogbookPanel`, `SettingsPanel`, `AchievementCard` (slides in from the right in the bottom right corner, above the buttons there; several earned at once take turns, and none slides in while a catch banner is hung out), `TacklePanel` (the bait on the hook and the rod in hand, each with a tip naming the fish here it suits; a rod suits the fish sizes in its `best` range in `gear.json`, and `Bite.rod_fit` makes smaller fish shy of it), `GuideNote` (old Rufus in the top right corner with the lesson for the coast or the town, an arrow at what to click from the screen's `guide_target(id)`, Got it and Skip the guide; the settings turn him back on), `Controls` (keys and controller), `Pixels` (lines, rings, discs and polygons drawn as whole game pixels, and `look()`, the shader that keeps a scaled or turned picture's pixels whole).
- `game/tests/`: `run_tests.gd` runs every `test_*` method in every `test_*.gd`; a test that raises an engine error fails. `pacing_bot.gd` and `flow_bot.gd` are the bots.
- `game/tools/style_tests/`: the two approved style tests, kept as references and never exported.
- `tools/art/`: `pixellab.py` (the token is in `.env`, never in code; every picture's cost goes in `ledger.csv`; `inpaint --tile WxH` sends tiles at least that size, so a thin area is painted seeing the picture round it, and its tiles start on multiples of 4, so an area should too), `make_places.py` with the specs written by `places/_write_specs.py` (land with a `style` picture is painted to match it, as the harbour's cape and the rocky shore are to `places/style_land.png`, a piece of the harbour town, and the harbour's hills and mountains, Lucrine's shores and Capri's cliffs are to the cape itself; ## Fixed decisions

- The game is laid out at 480 by 270 game pixels, each the largest whole number of window pixels that fits, as Farmus does (Godot's own stretch is off: its stretch modes show a maximised window at the size it opened at). `Nav.port` draws it at the window's resolution (`size_2d_override` with its stretch, and every corner snapped to a window pixel), so moving things slide by fractions of a game pixel while each texel stays whole. Nothing that moves is rounded to a game pixel; a line or shape is drawn with `Pixels`, and a picture scaled or turned with `Pixels.look()`. `Nav.sky` draws behind the game at the window's resolution too. It opens windowed; F11 switches to full screen; F10 the desktop strip.
- The interface (`Nav.ui` in `Nav.ui_layer`) is laid out in interface pixels from the window's top left corner, each `Nav.ui_zoom` window pixels: the game's zoom, or a step smaller or larger by the interface size in the settings (`Settings.interface_size`, -1, 0 or 1), never under 1, and larger only while the interface keeps 360 by 200 pixels (`Nav.UI_LEAST`; 60 rows in the strip). `Nav.ui_view()` is its size. Anything placed by the world goes through `Nav.to_ui()` or `Nav.rect_to_ui()` (view pixels, where the world is drawn on the screen, into interface pixels) and `Nav.from_ui()` back, as the speech bubbles, the town's `guide_target`, the plants kept clear of the buttons and the strip's clickable outline do. A window too big for the interface is drawn at the most window pixels per pixel that fit it (`Ui.fit_window`), and Godot's tips, which it draws at the game's zoom, are drawn again at the interface's (`Nav.fit_tip`).
- Every balance number lives in `game/data/*.json`, read with `Data.num("time.day_minutes")`. The chapters open at the names in `chapters.json`, tuned with the pacing bot.
- Art is painted with PixelLab without outlines, and no picture's edge may ever show as a straight cut: land carries on below the water and rock runs to the bottom of the world.
- A floor, wall or quay is one painted piece, joined onto itself when it repeats; pieces painted apart are never set side by side, and an old picture never gets new sides painted on. Check every scene by eye at game scale, since the edge script cannot see a change of texture.
- A texture is loaded before drawing starts; one first loaded inside a draw call shows as a blank square.
