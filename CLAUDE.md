# Fishus Maximus: Claude Code Guide

A cozy Roman fishing game in Godot 4.7 (GDScript, 2D, GL Compatibility), set on the Bay of Naples in AD 72. The design is in `Overview.md`, the detailed rules in `Mechanics.md`, and the build plan in `docs/2026-11-06-full-game.md`.

## Where things are

- `game/scripts/autoload/`: `Dev` (command-line options and screenshots), `Data` (everything in `game/data/*.json`), `Settings` (volumes for everything, music, effects and the ambience of sea, gulls and rain; the window, the interface size, kilograms or pounds (`units`, kilograms by default), gentle fishing, text size, the tips (`tooltips`, which `Tips` follows), the keys the player has changed (`keys`, see `Controls`)), `Game` (the game being played, one dictionary; keys starting with `_` and a fight in progress are never saved; `catch_up()` lets time away pass; `demo_state()` is a game some way in), `Saves` (three slots, saved every minute, on every change of screen and on close as Farmus saves: written to a `.tmp` and read back before it is moved into place, with three rolling backups `.bak1` to `.bak3` and an older game's single `.bak` still read; a game is never written over, so with every slot taken New game waits for one to be deleted), `Nav` (screens and the world and interface layers; Escape and the pad's B close the open window or else step back out of the screen through its `go_back`, never out of the game (`back_out`); `cover()` lays a screen's picture over the whole window at whole window pixels per picture pixel, keeping its 480 by 270 scene in view when the picture is painted a `margin` past it, so no mirrored copy shows), `Tips` (the tips, drawn in interface pixels as Mosaicus Maximus draws them), `Pad` (a controller works every menu as Mosaicus Maximus's does, a stick moving the pointer and A clicking; on the coast the left stick rows and A casts, so there the right stick moves the pointer and A clicks only on a button or in a window), `Awards` (achievements handed to Steam, with `run_callbacks` and every earned one sent again on each change of screen; never from a test or a made-up game), `Windowing` (the desktop strip, F10, its settings kept in the `[strip]` section of `settings.cfg` and carried over once from the old `window.json`), `Audio` (effects by file name, ambience loops, music: the game's own tracks and any the player puts in `user://music/`, which the settings open, read afresh before each track as Farmus does)).
- `game/scripts/rules/`: the rules as plain classes, tested headless. `World.advance` is the one clock: pots, freshness, helpers, and each new morning the market, orders, the pond, tanks, weather and the pond show. Also `Clock`, `Catalog`, `Bite`, `LineFight`, `Catches`, `Hold`, `Traps`, `Nets`, `Trident`, `Idle`, `Market`, `Orders`, `Story` (chapters and clients), `Shop`, `Ponds` (the estate's one fish pond in `state["pond"]`, dug and then widened stage by stage as `chapters.json` allows (`pond`), its room from `stages` in `ponds.json`; brackish fish need its `spring`; `Ponds.from_sites` turns an old save's six pond sites into the one pond, keeping every fish, feature and breeding count, the brackish site becoming the spring, dug as wide as the old ponds' room together; `Game.from_dict` does it for any save with `ponds`), `Garum`, `Weather`, `Events`, `Legends`, `Achievements`, `Helpers`, `Gossip`, `Dogs`, `Rescue` (old Rufus's worms for a player with no bait, no money for any and nothing to sell, as Uncle Marcus helps in Farmus; `start.rescue_worms`), and `Guide` (old Rufus's lessons for a new player, in `data/guide.json`: the first not yet learned is shown, each cleared by a count in `stats` or the logbook, and a lesson once learned stays learned).
- `game/scripts/coast/`: a fishing place seen side on. `coast_screen.gd` holds the line, the bite, the strike and the fight; `ParallaxLayers` slides each painted layer at its own speed, `FloorView` lays the sand strip along the seabed line, `WaterView` draws the sea by depth, `UnderLand` the land carried on under the water as two darker shades of it (the far slope under the nearest row of land, reaching the surface under its tall hills and falling away between them, and a nearer bank of mounds behind the seabed), `Murk` the murky water over everything under the waterline (drawn after the fish, it reads back the screen, mutes it a little toward a sea grey and lays the water's colour over it more with depth, so the water stays a sea; plants, pieces on the seabed and fish first lose most of their own colour in their shaders, by the shares in `Murk`, and the lamp's light, the line and the boat are drawn after it), `BoatView` the hull and the fisherman from `assets/art/fisherman/`: seated with his rod, turned to face the stern and rowing on a boat with `oars` in `boats.json`, each stroke splashing softly as the blade goes into the water (`stroke` in the sounds, cut by `cut_sounds.py`), the rod laid down in the boat while one without oars sails along (`rod_down()`, which also hides the hook), winding back and throwing to cast (every cast, the game's own and one let go early, swings the rest of the way back over his shoulder before it comes forward, the float hanging from the rod's tip until then), with the oar and the rod drawn in his hands (a boat whose sail or high sides hide the seat names `seat` and `dog` in `boats.json`: how far along, and the row; each boat's `rail` is its near rail, as points [column, row] in its picture, and `BoatView.front` draws the hull from that row down over him and the dog, so its far side, seen above the near rail where a stern or bow rises, stays behind them), a sailing boat's `sail` (its corners in the picture), `mast` (columns standing in front of the sail) and `flag` (the pole's column, the free end's column, the top and bottom rows) in `boats.json` are moved by whole texels in `sail.gdshader`: the flag always waves a little and more under way, and the sail bellies out towards the bow as the wind fills it (`BoatView.wind`), `DogView` the dog (drawn between the boat and the hull's near side so it sits down inside it, and cut off a little below the near rail so it never shows under the keel; sitting and lying down play once and hold), `Swimmers` the fish (each drawn one texel to a pixel, never scaled, so no rows double; after a place opens, new fish start out of sight past the view's sides, clear of it by their own half width, and swim in, and `bring_in` sends one kind in that way for a scripted moment, so no fish ever appears in view; a fish above the top of its depth sinks back to it gently rather than jumping; a biting fish puts its mouth, the front column of its picture (`mouth_of`), on the middle of the bait and follows the hook as the float drifts; a fish keeps to water at least `Catalog.water_needed()` deep, more for bigger fish, from `fishing.deep_water` in `balance.json`; a fish of a shoal takes a bait only `fishing.shoal_bite` as often as one alone, so the little shoal fish bite now and then without crowding out the rest; they spawn spread out along the water near the camera, `APART` from each other and fewer in the shallows (`room_for_fish`), no kind with more than `MOST_SHOALS` shoals or `MOST_SINGLES` lone fish near the camera (`crowded`), so no one kind fills the screen, each put where it swims at once; each wanders in curves, changes pace, pauses and eases round to turn, its tail waved by `Pixels.look`; `wave`, `waves` and `leaps` in `fish.json` set a kind apart), `LineView` the line, the float and bars, each bait drawn on the hook as a few pixels of its own (`BAITS`) until a fish takes it into its mouth (`bait_shown()`), the hooked fish and its leaps clear of the water, `CoastMethods` pots, net, trident and fishing on his own (a fish he catches is kept at once, then shown taking the float under and coming in on the line before it is told of), `BoatLantern` the big boat's lantern (its flame at `lantern` in `boats.json`), wavering and added onto the scene outside the night's darkening, so it lights the prow and lies on the water in the dark; `TrapView` the pots (one set is lifted over the boat's side and sinks to the seabed swaying, trailing bubbles and raising a puff of silt, and rests leaning with the sand; one hauled comes up hand over hand to the boat and its catch is told once it is in; the view goes down with it unless he is rowing; the game sets or lifts a pot at once and only the picture takes its time, so a save meanwhile is safe), `CoastEvents` dolphins, the tuna haul and the Neptunalia, `SkyView` the sky by the hour in `Nav.sky` (Farmus's banded gradient, sun, moon and stars, and its clouds from `assets/art/sky/` in three depths, more and greyer when it is overcast; Farmus's northern lights on rare clear nights (`aurora.gdshader`, `sky.aurora_chance` in `balance.json`, `--aurora` or `--aurora=rising`); flocks of gulls by day and swallows at dusk; `SkyPalette` holds the colours), `CoastCamera` the camera (it rises while he rows so the view looks over the water, rises with a flying float, and keeps the float on screen).
- A place in `game/data/places.json` lists its `seabed` line, its `floor` strip, `ground_below` (below the seabed plus this the ground is solid shadow), its `map` pin and its layers: `file` (under `assets/art`), `at` (on screen with the camera at home, the waterline 160 pixels down) or `world` (world pixels, the waterline at 0), `factor`, `z`, `mirror`, `alpha`, `repeat`, `through` (crosses the waterline, so its lower part is tinted), or `band` (the sea under a row of land, shading into the band behind it). Each band of sea is drawn by `sea.gdshader`, mirroring the sky's colours for the hour and weather from `SkyView.look()`, with the sun's or moon's path on it; each land picture over a band draws its own reflection with `reflection.gdshader`, starting at its waterline per column (`ParallaxLayers.waterlines()`), darker and tinted toward the band, broken by ripples that grow in rain. A `front` layer (`"top"` or `"bottom"`) is a dark plant right in front of the view from `assets/art/front/`, hanging from the view's top edge or standing up from its bottom one on its own bank of near shore, the bank's waterline (`ParallaxLayers.FRONT_DEPTH` rows above the picture's bottom) kept on the sea's and its foot below the view: `x` is the place along the coast it stands in front of and `factor` (above 1) how much faster than the boat it slides. It breaks away in blocks of two pixels (`front.gdshader`, Farmus's market dissolve) while the boat, the float, a fish fight or a button along the bottom of the screen is behind it (`ParallaxLayers.keep_clear()`), and comes back the same way; a branch rises out of the view as it dips into the water, a plant at the bottom breaks away then, and none shows in the desktop strip. A layer with `sway` (seagrass, kelp, reeds and the like) sways by whole texels in `sway.gdshader`. Pieces at z -9 stand behind the floor; the floor hides their bases. A piece set in the water with `world` is drawn dim and with little of its own colour (`Murk.shade_for`, `bed.gdshader`, and `sway.gdshader` for plants).
- `game/scripts/town/`, `shore/`, `map/`, `screens/`: the town street and its panels, the estate's pond and garum works, the bay map (its picture over the plain deep sea of its edges, fading into it on each side where the view reaches past it, with `Backdrop`), and the title (`title.gd`: the harbour at sunset built from the coast's own pieces, its sky, layers, plants in front, water, land under the water, floor, murk and fish, with the camera held still and centred in a view of any shape, so it always looks as the game does; it runs before a game is loaded, so it reads nothing from `Game.state` and its `Swimmers` have `with_legends` off; a new game chooses the dog as Farmus does, its breed and coat with arrows and its name, each breed's three or more coats recoloured from its own fur by `Dogs.recolour` and kept in `dog`, `dog_coat` and `dog_name`; deleting a save asks for the word typed in a window of its own). The town lays one picture painted past its 480 by 270 scene (`street.png`) over the window with `Nav.cover(margin)`, the margin read from the picture's size and the picture drawn at minus the margin so the scene keeps its own coordinates; no mirrored copies. It shows the coast's live sky through its cleared sky with `AshoreSky` (a `SkyView` in `Nav.sky` by the hour and the weather, its horizon kept on the screen's `HORIZON` row), and lights the picture and the people by `SkyView.light(hour)`, dimmed in rain, while the gold outline and the names over them stay bright. The estate (`shore_screen.gd`, `--screen=shore`) is one large pond seen side on as the coast is, built from the coast's own pieces as the title is: its `SkyView`, bands of sea mirroring the sky (`LAYERS`, in scene rows, laid out `RAISE` rows higher because the coast's pieces expect the waterline at row 160, and the sea's shaders' `home` moved by `SKY_SHIFT` so their horizon is the scene's row 92), the harbour's far row across the bay, a beach, the pond's far bank (`assets/art/estate/bank.png`: the villa on its rise, the garum works and a grassy rise behind one long stone wall, mirrored in the pond), and under the pond's waterline at row 182 the coast's `WaterView`, a floor strip (sand with the `sand` feature, rock without), the features' pieces on the floor (`PIECES`: the pond-sized rocks and den, seagrass, tufts; the spring, the channel, the tunnel and the shade drawn on the far wall), the fish and the `Murk`, so it shares the coast's light, time of day and water. The sea washes up the beach in `wash.gdshader` on the beach's sand picture, which draws the same water as the bands: each wave breaks white just offshore, runs up the sand in a lighter sheet with a lip of foam, slows, and draws back, leaving lace at its highest reach and dark wet sand that dries over a few seconds, each reaching the beach a moment later further along; `ShoreWash` holds its rhythm (a wave every `PERIOD` seconds, the shares surging and drawing back, the backwash) and hands it to the shader. `PondView` swims the pond's fish one texel to a pixel, wandering, pausing and turning as the coast's do; Feed brings them up to the food and the bell lines the named ones up across the front with their names over them in the interface. The mouse goes by its own events (`_mouse`), so a scripted `--click` or `--hover` finds the pond or the garum works, whose gold outline and hit test follow `garum_mask.png`. The street is a covered hall: its beam rests on the hall's wooden post at the left and the stone pillar at the right, the fish market, the netmaker, the boatwright and the goldsmith stand as stalls along the wall between them, and the harbour board stands at the far left on the paving in front of the left post, turned to face the street; the townsfolk turn back before it (`WALK`). The town's lamps at `LAMPS` (the lantern in the goldsmith's stall, the hall's lantern on its left post and the oil lamp under the fish market's awning) are each a `Lamp`, as Farmus's are: a `PointLight2D` with a soft round texture that lights the street and the people round it, and an additive halo over the flame, outside the lit layer so the night does not dim it. They burn faintly by day and brightest at night or in rain, each flickering on its own; the outline and names keep `light_mask` 0, so the lamplight leaves them as they are. A building in the town is found by the mouse on its cut-out's opaque pixels, and its gold outline is every pixel touching those on a side or a corner, past the picture's edges too, so it runs unbroken all the way round.

## Fixed decisions

- The game is laid out at 480 by 270 game pixels, each the largest whole number of window pixels that fits, as Farmus does (Godot's own stretch is off: its stretch modes show a maximised window at the size it opened at). `Nav.port` draws it at the window's resolution (`size_2d_override` with its stretch, and every corner snapped to a window pixel), so moving things slide by fractions of a game pixel while each texel stays whole. Nothing that moves is rounded to a game pixel; a line or shape is drawn with `Pixels`, and a picture scaled or turned with `Pixels.look()`. `Nav.sky` draws behind the game at the window's resolution too. It opens windowed; F11 switches to full screen; F10 the desktop strip.
- The interface (`Nav.ui` in `Nav.ui_layer`) is laid out in interface pixels from the window's top left corner, each `Nav.ui_zoom` window pixels: a step from the game's zoom by the interface size in the settings (`Settings.ui_scale_step`: -1 Smaller, two steps under the game's zoom, 0 Normal, a step under it and the default, 1 Larger, the game's zoom, or 2 Largest, a step over it; an old `interface_size` is read one step up, so it draws the same), never under 1, and larger only while the interface keeps 360 by 200 pixels (`Nav.UI_LEAST`; 60 rows in the strip). `Nav.ui_view()` is its size. Anything placed by the world goes through `Nav.to_ui()` or `Nav.rect_to_ui()` (view pixels, where the world is drawn on the screen, into interface pixels) and `Nav.from_ui()` back, as the speech bubbles, the town's `guide_target`, the plants kept clear of the buttons and the strip's clickable outline do. A window too big for the interface is drawn at the most window pixels per pixel that fit it (`Ui.fit_window`), and the tips are drawn by the `Tips` autoload in interface pixels, as Mosaicus Maximus draws them, from text given with `Ui.tip_on` (never Godot's `tooltip_text`), broken into lines by width and placed beside the pointer by `Nav.tip_at`. The catch banner alone is scaled back to the game's zoom (`CatchCard`), so its fish and cloth show in the scene's own pixels.
- Every shown string goes through `tr()`, or `TranslationServer.translate()` in a static function; labels and buttons also translate a whole literal by themselves, and `Toast.say` and the tips translate what they are given. A format is translated before it is filled (`tr("Hold %d/%d") % [...]`), never the filled text. A name is `Data.name_of(entry)`, `Data.fish_name(id)`, and inside a sentence `Data.lower_of(entry)` or `Data.fish_lower(id)`, a row of its own (`lower` in the data where lower-casing the first letter is wrong, as for John Dory), since other languages change the words round it; lists are joined with `Ui.list` ("a, b and c") or `Ui.series` ("a, b, c"), sentences with `Ui.text([...])`, and headings put in capitals with `Ui.upper`, which translates first and keeps the Turkish dotted capital i. Words kept to show later are `Ui.say(format, parts)`, shown with `Ui.text()`, so they read in whatever language is chosen then; the game keeps no translated text in its saves. Old Rufus's lessons write a key as `{cast}`, `{row_left}` and so on, filled with the key chosen now by `Controls.fill`; key names stay in English.
- Settings has a Language list on its Gameplay tab, each language named in its own words and letters (`SettingsPanel.NATIVE_NAMES`), kept as `language` in the `[play]` section of `settings.cfg`. Until one is chosen the computer's language is used if the game has it (`Settings.system_language`), else English. Choosing one rebuilds the screen (`Nav.reopen`) with the settings open again.
- Fonts (`UiTheme`): text is Fusion Pixel 10 px, which has Polish, Turkish and Cyrillic letters; Chinese, Japanese and Korean use Fusion Pixel's 8 px fonts at 8 px; headings are Silkscreen in English, French, German, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese and the text font otherwise, and the game's name is always Silkscreen (`UiTheme.name_font`). Sizes come from `UiTheme.text_size()` and `heading_size()`, never fixed numbers; a name drawn over the scene uses `Ui.draw_tag`. Text drawn by hand that breaks into lines (`Tips`, the logbook's two-line entries) breaks between letters in Chinese and Japanese.
- Every balance number lives in `game/data/*.json`, read with `Data.num("time.day_minutes")`. The chapters open at the names in `chapters.json`, tuned with the pacing bot.
- Art is painted with PixelLab without outlines, and no picture's edge may ever show as a straight cut: land carries on below the water and rock runs to the bottom of the world.
- A floor, wall or quay is one painted piece, joined onto itself when it repeats; pieces painted apart are never set side by side, and an old picture never gets new sides painted on. Check every scene by eye at game scale, since the edge script cannot see a change of texture.
- A texture is loaded before drawing starts; one first loaded inside a draw call shows as a blank square.
