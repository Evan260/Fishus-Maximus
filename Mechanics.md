# Fishus Maximus: mechanics and features

The rules of the game in detail, building on `Overview.md`. Money in the game is in denarii.

## Time

- **A day** runs 20 minutes from dawn to dusk, then a short night of 5 minutes. The light changes through dawn, morning, midday, afternoon, the sunset and night, and each hour has its own fish.
- **A season** is 7 days and **a year** is 28: spring, summer, autumn and winter. The game starts on the first morning of spring, AD 72.
- **Sleeping** at the cottage by the garum works skips the rest of the night.
- **While the game is closed**, up to 8 hours count:
  - the traps fill;
  - the garum ferments;
  - the pond fish grow and are fed if a feeder is hired;
  - the boat goes on fishing if it was left fishing on its own.

## Places

The bay is six fishing places. Each is one long stretch of coast, 3 to 5 screens wide and 1 to 3 screens deep. You row along a place, and sail between places on the bay map, which takes an hour of the day.

| Place | Reached with | Depth | Known for |
|---|---|---|---|
| The harbour | the skiff | 1 screen | The town and the quay; mullet, bream and sardines over sand and seagrass; octopus under the quay |
| The rocky shore | the skiff | 1.5 | Morays in the rocks, wrasse, scorpionfish, sea urchins, lobsters for the traps |
| The Lucrine Lake | the fishing boat | 1, brackish | The oyster beds Sergius Orata laid out at Baiae, eels, grey mullet, sea bass |
| The open bay | the fishing boat | 2 | Mackerel, bonito, sea bass, the tuna shoals in spring, the dolphins |
| The sea caves of Capri | the sailing boat | 2, dim | Parrotfish, cave fish, rare fish at night |
| Off Cape Misenum | the big boat | 3 | Groupers, conger eels, amberjack, swordfish, and most of the legends |

## The camera

- **Along the coast,** the camera follows the boat and leads it a little in the direction of travel, so you see where you are rowing.
- **Down the line,** it follows the hook as it sinks. The waterline rises up the screen and off the top, and comes back down as the line is reeled in.
- **In layers.** Every place is drawn in parallax layers that slide at their own speeds:
  - **Above water:** the sky and the sun; the far mountains and Vesuvius; the far coast; headlands with villas and umbrella pines; the near shore with the town, cliffs or caves.
  - **At the waterline:** the quay, rocks breaking the surface, and the boat.
  - **Below water:** reefs fading into the blue haze; the seabed and its rock; seagrass and weed in front of the fish.
- **The light** comes from the hour and the weather. Water deepens from light green near the surface to dark blue near the bottom, and fish take on the colour of the water they swim in. At night the lamp on the bow lights a cone of water.

## Fishing with a line

1. **Bait.** Choose the bait before you cast: worm, shrimp, bread, a strip of sardine, squid, a live sardine, or a special bait for a legend. Every fish has the baits it likes, and on the wrong bait it rarely bites.
2. **Cast.** Hold to build up the cast and let go. The float lands that far from the boat and the hook starts to sink.
3. **Depth.** The hook sinks until you stop it or it reaches the seabed. The line's length sets how deep it can go.
4. **A bite.** A fish that likes the bait comes to look, swims in to the hook and nibbles: the float twitches one to three times. Then it takes the bait, the float goes under and the dog barks. Strike within two seconds.
   - Strike while it only nibbles and it darts off, but the line stays in the water with its bait.
   - Too late, and the fish takes the bait and goes.
   - Pressing with no fish at the hook reels the line in.
5. **The fight.** A bar over the hooked fish shows the line's tension, and turns red as the line nears breaking. The fish runs and rests by turns until it tires. Running, it faces away, thrashes and takes line; resting, it lets itself be reeled in.
   - Reeling while it runs strains the starting line to breaking point within about half a second, so a run means let go.
   - Giving line while it runs is always safe.
   - Reeling while it rests brings it in.
   - Leaving the line slack for a couple of seconds lets the hook slip.
   - Big and strong fish run more often and for longer.
   - **Gear:** the rod tires the fish and reels faster, a stronger line takes longer to break, and a better hook holds a slack line for longer.
   - **Gentle fishing,** in the settings, never snaps a line or lets a fish slip.
6. **Landing.** The fish comes up with the camera, and goes into the hold, or into the live well if there is room and you want it alive.

## Traps

- Wicker pots are dropped from the boat onto the seabed wherever you choose, and fill over the hours with the place's trap fish: octopus, lobsters, crabs and eels.
- You can have 2 traps at the start and up to 6 later. They are pulled up whenever you row over them.
- Trap catches are alive.

## Nets

- **The cast net** is thrown over a shoal you can see near the surface: aim a ring over it and throw. It catches several small fish at once, such as sardines, anchovies, bogue and mackerel.
- **Net size** grows with upgrades.
- **What netted fish are for:** they die in the net, so they go to the market, the garum works or the bait box.

## The trident

- At night only, with the lamp lit on the bow.
- Fish come to the light. You aim and throw, leading a moving fish a little.
- The trident takes the night fish: cuttlefish, squid, octopus and flatfish on the sand.
- Speared fish are dead.

## Fishing on its own

- Switching to idle lets the fisherman fish on his own, with the bait and depth you last used, at about a third of the active rate.
- It never takes a legend or a live fish, and it stops when the hold is full.
- The traps keep working throughout.
- **The desktop strip** from Farmus Maximus shows the boat and its line on a band of water along the bottom of the screen, fishing on its own.

## The hold, the live well and freshness

- **The hold** carries dead fish, from 10 baskets in the skiff to 40 in the big boat.
- **Freshness:** fresh fish lose value through the day. Salting them in the hold keeps them, but salted fish sell for less. Whatever is left at dusk can go to the garum works.
- **The live well** is a tank in the boat for live fish, from 2 in the fishing boat to 6 in the big boat. Live fish must reach a pond or a live order the same day.

## The fish

About sixty kinds, each with:
- the places, depths, hours, seasons and weather it is found in;
- the baits it likes;
- its weight range, strength and rarity;
- its price per pound;
- whether it can live in a pond, and what it needs there;
- what it is worth to the garum works.

**Size.** Every fish caught has its own weight within its kind's range, in four grades: small, good, fine and prize. Prize fish sell for many times a small one. Weights are shown in kilograms, or in pounds when the settings say so.

**The logbook** has a page for every kind:
- **Seen:** once a fish has been seen swimming, its page shows its outline.
- **Caught:** once caught, the page shows its picture, where and when it was found, its record weight, and how it was caught.
- **Rewards:** filling pages earns small rewards from the town.

### The twelve legends

Each legend has a clue told in town, and turns up only under its own conditions:

1. **The octopus of Carteia**, which raids traps on dark nights. Catch it in the act with a trap baited with salted fish.
2. **Tiberius's mullet**, a red mullet of four and a half pounds, at dawn in the harbour in autumn.
3. **The moray of Hortensius**, old and enormous, in a cave at Capri. It comes to its name.
4. **The great tuna**, in the open bay during the tuna run.
5. **Orata's golden bream**, in the Lucrine Lake at noon in summer.
6. **Claudius's parrotfish**, the first of the parrotfish the emperor had brought to these waters, at Capri.
7. **The swordfish of Misenum**, off the cape on stormy days.
8. **The Senator**, a grouper as old as the town, deep off the cape.
9. **The conger of the wreck**, at night, at the old wreck off the rocky shore.
10. **The numbing ray**, whose touch makes the arm go numb, on the sand of the open bay.
11. **The sleeping sunfish**, which lies flat on the surface on calm summer afternoons.
12. **Neptune's lobster**, in the deepest cave at Capri.

## The ponds

The heart of the game: a living collection you build, breed and show off.

- **Building.** Up to six ponds are cut into the rocks along the shore, each small or large. Seawater ponds come first; the Lucrine pond is brackish.
- **Features** a pond can be given:
  - rocks;
  - caves for morays;
  - weed and sand;
  - a shade roof;
  - a channel for fresh sea water, the last of which is a tunnel cut through the rock as Lucullus did for his.
- **Fish needs.** Each kind needs its water, its features, room and food. A pond that suits a fish keeps it happy, and a happy fish grows and breeds.
- **Feeding.** Fish are fed once a day: by hand with scraps and sardines, or by a hired feeder.
- **Growth.** Pond fish gain weight every day up to their kind's limit. Grown in a good pond, they reach sizes never caught wild.
- **Breeding.** Two happy adults of the same kind have young after a few days.
- **Pets.** Any pond fish can be named:
  - **Calling:** ring the pond's bell and named fish swim to the front.
  - **Dressing up:** they can be decked out with finery from the goldsmith, earrings included, as Antonia's moray was.
- **Visitors.** Villa owners and townsfolk come to see the ponds, and fine ponds raise your name in town.
- **The pond show.** Once a year the town's pond keepers show their best fish for prizes.
- **Selling live.** Pond fish fill live orders and sell to the villas at Baiae.

## The garum works

- **The tanks.** One of the eight stone tanks works at the start. The rest are restored with denarii and stone.
- **Filling a tank.** A tank takes fish by weight and salt by measure, then ferments for days. Stirring it each day raises the quality; a hired helper can do it.
- **The grades,** by the fish used and the days left:
  - **Allec,** the paste left at the bottom, after 3 days.
  - **Muria,** the brine, after 5.
  - **Garum,** after 10.
  - **Garum sociorum,** the finest, made from mackerel only, after 20.
- **Selling.** Garum sells at the market every day, or to the export ship every seventh day for more, once it is in amphorae.
- **The buyer.** Umbricius Scaurus, the great garum merchant of Pompeii, buys the finest.

## The harbour town

You row back to the quay to trade. The town is its own scene, a street of buildings to click:

- **The fish market,** the *macellum*, with its round fish hall and water basin.
  - Prices depend on the kind and the size.
  - Each kind's price drops as more of it is sold that day, so a varied catch pays best.
  - The salt seller has a stall here.
- **The netmaker:** rods, lines, hooks, bait, nets, traps and lamps.
- **The boatwright's yard:** the four boats.
- **The harbour board:** the day's orders.
- **The goldsmith:** finery for named pond fish.

The townsfolk are named people with their own trades. Their talk carries the clues to the legends and to where fish are biting.

## Orders and clients

- **The harbour board** has three orders each morning, and more as your name grows. An order names a kind of fish, a minimum weight, alive or not, and a deadline of the same day or a few days. It pays in denarii and raises your name.
- **Clients.** Six named clients come back with their own stories:
  - a senator building ponds at Baiae, who wants rare live fish;
  - the cook of a Pompeii tavern, who needs fish for the day's dishes;
  - Trimalchio, the freedman from Mosaicus Maximus, with ever more absurd banquets;
  - Umbricius Scaurus, the garum merchant;
  - the priest of Neptune, for the god's offerings;
  - a rich widow with the finest ponds on the bay, your rival at the pond show.

## Your name and the chapters

Your name in town rises with orders, the pond show and legends caught. Five chapters open the game up:

1. **The harbour:** the skiff, the line, the market and the first pond.
2. **The rocky shore:** traps, morays and the first restored garum tanks.
3. **The lake and the bay:** the fishing boat, nets, the tuna run and more ponds.
4. **Capri:** the sailing boat, the trident and night fishing.
5. **The deep:** the big boat, Cape Misenum and the hunt for the legends.

## Boats and upgrades

- **The four boats:**
  - **The skiff:** two oars, a hold of 10.
  - **The fishing boat:** a hold of 20 and a live well of 2.
  - **The sailing boat:** faster sailing, a hold of 30, a well of 4.
  - **The big boat:** a hold of 40, a well of 6 and a lamp post.
- **Gear:** four rods, four lines, three hooks, three nets, traps from 2 to 6, two lamps and a bigger bait box.
- **On shore:** six ponds and their features, eight garum tanks, a salt store and an amphora store.
- **Helpers:**
  - a boy who pulls the traps and stirs the tanks;
  - later, a second fisherman, who fishes on his own while you do something else.

## Weather, seasons and events

- **Weather:**
  - clear;
  - cloudy;
  - rain, when more fish bite;
  - wind, when the waves make nets harder;
  - storms, which keep the boats in harbour and leave rare fish in the shallows afterwards.
- **The tuna run** fills one week of spring. Watchers on the headland shout when the shoals come in, and the whole town nets the bay together.
- **The Neptunalia,** on 23 July, holds a fishing contest in the harbour.
- **The Saturnalia** in winter brings the year's biggest orders for feasts.
- **Dolphin days:** some days a pod of dolphins herds fish towards the nets.
- **Night** belongs to the trident, the lamp and the night fish.

## The dog

- The dog sits in the bow, barks at a bite and wags at a shoal below. It can be petted, and naps in the boat while you fish on your own.
- The dogs are the Farmus Maximus dogs, chosen at the start.

## Collection and goals

- The logbook, its records and the twelve legends.
- The ponds, and the named fish in them.
- About 40 Steam achievements: every chapter, every legend, a full logbook, a prize fish of every kind, every pond built and every tank restored, a win at the pond show and at the Neptunalia, and a named moray in earrings.

## The look

- Pixel art at 480 by 270, scaled up by whole numbers. The art is painted with PixelLab.
- **No edge ever shows.** No picture's edge is ever visible as a straight line where land, rock or a wall stops:
  - each piece is painted to end in a natural shape, or blended into its neighbour with inpainting;
  - rock and sand run down to the bottom of the world;
  - land above the water carries on below it.
  - A script checks every picture's edges before a scene is shown.
- **One style:**
  - every sprite is painted without outlines, matching the scenery;
  - fish are tinted by the depth they swim at;
  - everything shares the scene's light.

## Sound and music

- **Sound:** lapping water, oars, the creak of the boat, the reel's click, splashes, the dog, gulls by day, cicadas at sunset, the bustle of the market, rain and wind.
- **Music:** calm, on lyre and pipes, quieter at night.

## Interface

- **On screen:** denarii, the day, the season and the weather, how full the hold and the live well are, the bait and the fishing method. No clock ticks on screen: the sky tells the hour.
- **Panels:** the shops, the logbook, the bay map, the ponds and the garum works.

## Platform, controls and saving

- **Platform:** Steam, on Windows, Mac, Linux and Steam Deck. Built in Godot 4 on Farmus Maximus's systems.
- **Controls:**
  - **Mouse:** click to cast, strike and reel. A and D row.
  - **Controller:** full support.
- **Saving:** three save slots, kept with Steam Cloud.
- **Settings:** gentle fishing, text size, weights in kilograms or pounds (kilograms by default), the desktop strip, and fishing on its own.
