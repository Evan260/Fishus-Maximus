# Fishus Maximus

A cozy fishing game set on the Bay of Naples in AD 72. You fish from a small boat, but the Romans were mad about fish, and the catch is only the start. The best fish are kept alive in seaside ponds for rich villa owners, the rest go to the market or into your garum works, and the whole town orders fish for its dinners and banquets. Fish by hand, or leave the boat fishing on its own in a strip along your desktop.

What sets it apart from other fishing games is the ponds: a living collection you build, breed and show off, the way the Romans did.

The detailed rules are in `Mechanics.md`.

## The setting

Your uncle has left you his fishing boat, a tumbledown garum works on the shore and three empty fish ponds cut into the rocks. It is the same harbour town as Mosaicus Maximus, with Vesuvius green and quiet behind it, seven years before it erupts.

The tone is warm and a little funny, and the jokes are true:

- A three-pound red mullet sells for more than a slave.
- Antonia, the emperor's grandmother, hung earrings on her favourite moray eel.
- The orator Crassus wore mourning when his pet moray died.
- The fish in the emperor's ponds came when they were called by name.

## A day

1. **Morning:** set your traps, check the day's orders on the harbour board, and choose where to sail.
2. **On the water:** fish by hand, or switch to idle and let the boat fish on its own. The day passes in real time, about twenty minutes from dawn to dusk.
3. **Back in harbour:** sell at the market, fill orders, put live fish in your ponds, and send the rest to the garum works.
4. **Night:** fish by lamplight with a trident for the night fish, or rest. The garum ferments and the ponds grow while you are away.

## Fishing

There are four ways to fish, each with its own feel:

- **Line:** the rod and hook, for single fish. Cast, wait, and reel with a short timing game: a fish pulls, you give line, it tires, you bring it in.
- **Traps:** wicker pots set in the morning and pulled up later, for octopus, lobsters and eels.
- **Nets:** a cast net over a shoal you can see from the boat, for many small fish at once.
- **Trident:** at night, by the light of a lamp on the bow, for fish that only come out in the dark.

Each place has its own fish, by time of day, season and weather:

- the harbour;
- the rocky shore;
- the open bay;
- the sea caves of Capri;
- the oyster beds of the Lucrine Lake;
- the deep water off the headland.

## The fish

About sixty kinds, all ones the Romans knew and wrote about. They include red mullet, gilt-head bream, sea bass, moray eels, parrotfish (which the emperor Claudius had shipped in to stock Italian waters), tuna, octopus, cuttlefish, sea urchins and oysters.

Each kind has its own sizes, and the biggest are worth far more.

Twelve legendary fish come from the old stories:

- the giant octopus of Carteia, which crept ashore at night to steal salted fish;
- the four-and-a-half-pound mullet that Tiberius sold at auction;
- a moray so old it answers to its name.

The logbook keeps every fish you have caught, with its record size.

## The ponds

Seaside fish ponds were the Romans' great fancy, and they are the heart of this game.

- **Live fish.** A fish caught on a line or in a trap can be kept alive in the boat's well and put in a pond.
- **Growing and breeding.** Fish grow in the ponds, and pairs breed. A pond's water, rocks and weed decide which fish thrive in it.
- **Building ponds.** You build them in the rocks along the shore, and add channels to bring in fresh sea water, shady caves for morays and beds for oysters.
- **Pet fish.** Your favourites can be named, fed by hand and dressed up, earrings included. Named fish come when they are called.
- **Orders.** Rich villa owners at Baiae order live fish for their own ponds, and pay most for rare ones grown large.

## The garum works

Garum, the fish sauce the Romans put on everything, is made from the fish nobody orders. Fish and salt go into stone tanks and ferment over days, including while the game is closed.

Better salt, more tanks and patience make better grades, from cheap sauce for the market to the finest, which ships take to Rome. Restoring your uncle's works, tank by tank, is the long project of the game.

## Orders and the town

The harbour board fills each morning with orders from the town:

- a mullet over three pounds for a senator's dinner tonight;
- live bream for a new pond at a villa;
- a hundred oysters for a wedding.

Orders pay far better than the market and raise your name in town, which brings bigger clients. Named clients come back with their own stories. The freedman Trimalchio, who ordered a floor from Mosaicus Maximus, now wants a fish for his banquet that nobody has ever seen.

## The year

The seasons turn, and each brings its own fish and events:

- **The tuna run in late spring:** watchers on the headland shout when the shoals come, and the whole town puts out its nets together.
- **The Neptunalia on 23 July:** a fishing contest in the harbour.
- **Storms in winter:** they keep the boats in, but wash rare fish into the shallows afterwards.
- **A pod of dolphins in the bay:** some days they herd fish towards your nets, as Pliny wrote they did for the fishermen of Gaul.

## Upgrades

- **The boat:** it grows from a rowing skiff to a fishing boat with a sail and a live well, and reaches further places as it does.
- **Gear:** better rods, lines, nets, traps and lamps.
- **On shore:** more ponds and better ones, more garum tanks, and a salt store.

A helper can be hired to pull your traps and stir the tanks while you fish.

## Your dog

A dog sits in the bow and barks when a fish bites. The dogs are the Farmus Maximus dogs, as in Mosaicus Maximus.

## What it looks like

Warm pixel art at 480 by 270, scaled up to fill the screen, in the series' style. The view is from the side, cut through at the waterline:

- **Above the water:** the boat, the harbour, the town and Vesuvius.
- **Below it:** the fish themselves, swimming over sand, rock and weed, so you see what you are fishing for.

Each stretch of coast is several screens wide and two deep, and the camera follows you:

- **Along the coast:** the camera follows the boat as you row along. The harbour, the open bay, the rocky cape and the sea caves are one long stretch of water.
- **Down the line:** when you cast, it follows the hook down. The waterline rises up the screen and off the top, and the deep water opens up, darker, with bigger fish. Reeling in brings it all back up.

Everything is drawn in many parallax layers that slide at their own speeds as the camera moves:

- **Above water:** the sky; Vesuvius and the far coast; headlands with villas and umbrella pines; the harbour town and the cape nearer still.
- **At the waterline:** the quay and the boat.
- **Below water:** reefs fading into the blue haze, the seabed, and seagrass in front of the fish.

The land carries on below the waterline: the quay goes down as a stone wall, and the shore as rock and sand.

Dawn, dusk, night, rain and storms all light the scene differently. The ponds and the garum works are scenes of their own on the shore.

The desktop strip shows the boat and its line along the bottom of the screen, with the water below.

## The harbour town

You row back to the pier to trade. The town is its own scene, a street of buildings to click:

- **The fish market,** the *macellum*, where the catch is sold. Pompeii's real one had a round fish hall in the middle with a water basin, and fish scales were found there.
- **The netmaker,** for rods, lines, hooks, nets, traps and lamps.
- **The boatwright's yard,** for a bigger boat, a sail and a live well.
- **The harbour board,** with the day's orders.
- **The garum works and the fish ponds,** along the shore at the edge of town.

## The look comes first

The first thing made is one finished scene, in the game, with real PixelLab art: the boat on the bay at sunset, with fish swimming below. The second is the same coast as a wide, deep scene in parallax layers, with the camera rowing along and following the hook down. Both are approved before any rules are written. The game is built only once they look good enough to sell it.

## Platform and controls

- **Where:** Steam, on Windows, Mac, Linux and Steam Deck.
- **Demo:** free, with chapters 1 and 2, the harbour and the rocky shore. It ends with a thank you, a glimpse of Capri, Misenum, the legends and the big boat, and a button to wishlist the full game, and its game carries over to the full game.
- **Controls:** played with the mouse, with full controller support.
- **Built with:** Godot 4 in 2D, on Farmus Maximus's systems: time passing while the game is closed, the desktop strip, saving and the trimmed engine.
