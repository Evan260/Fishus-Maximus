DESCRIPTION (3,509 of 4,000 characters)

Your uncle has left you his old boat. The bay is full of fish.

Fishus Maximus is a cozy fishing game set on the Bay of Naples in AD 72, in a little harbour town with Vesuvius green and quiet behind it. Row out at dawn, watch the fish swim under your boat, and bring them in one by one. The Romans were mad about fish, and the catch is only the start: keep the finest alive in your own pond, fill the town's orders, and make garum from the rest.

Free to start, one purchase for the whole game
Download Fishus Maximus for free and fish the harbour and the rocky shore through the first two chapters. One purchase unlocks the whole bay: the oyster beds of the Lucrine Lake, the open bay, the sea caves of Capri and the deep water off Cape Misenum, with bigger boats, more bait and gear, and a wider pond. Your game carries straight on. No ads, ever, and nothing else for sale.

Made for touch
Hold a finger on the water and lift it to cast, tap to strike, and hold to reel. Slide up or down the water to set your bait's depth, and hold the arrows at either side of the screen to row. Aim the net and the trident with your finger and let go to throw. Old Rufus's lessons show you each one as you go.

A rod, a float and a fish that fights back
• The view is cut through at the waterline, so you see every fish you are fishing for: what comes to look at your bait, what nibbles, and what finally takes it.
• Wait for the float to go under, strike, then give line while the fish runs and reel while it rests. Big fish leap clear of the water.
• Every catch is hung out on a painted linen banner with its weight and grade, and a starfish when it is new to your logbook.
• Four ways to fish: the rod and line, wicker pots set on the seabed, a cast net over a shoal, and the trident at night by the light of the lamp.
• Gentle fishing in the settings keeps the line from snapping, for a calmer evening.

Sixty kinds of fish, all ones the Romans knew
Red mullet, gilt-head bream, moray eels, octopus, tuna, swordfish and many more, each with its own places, hours, seasons, weather and favourite bait. Your logbook keeps every fish you catch and your best weight for each. Twelve legends come from the old stories, from the giant octopus of Carteia to a moray so old it answers to its name. The townsfolk drop the clues.

A pond of your own
Dig a fish pond into the rock by the beach and widen it as the chapters open. Give it rocks, caves for the morays, weed, sand, shade and a freshwater spring. Feed your fish, watch them grow and breed, and name your favourites. Ring the bell and the named ones swim to the front.

The harbour town
• Sell your catch at the fish market, where a varied catch pays best.
• Fill orders from the harbour board and from six regular clients with stories of their own, Trimalchio and his banquets among them.
• Restore your uncle's garum works tank by tank and make the sauce the Romans put on everything.
• Save for a bigger boat, from the rowing skiff to the big boat with its lantern.

Little joys
• Choose your dog's breed, coat and name. It rides in the boat, barks at a bite and wags when you pet it.
• Set the fisherman fishing on his own, and he keeps at it while you are away.
• Dolphins, the tuna run in spring, the Neptunalia fishing contest in July and the pond show.
• Calm harp music, lapping water, gulls by day and cicadas at dusk.
• 42 achievements, three saves, and play in 14 languages.

No timers and no failure. Just a quiet Roman bay and whatever is biting today.

APP PRIVACY

Data Not Collected is the true answer. The iPhone and iPad build's only native plugin is SlowSundayStore, which asks
StoreKit for the full game's price, buys it and restores it; the purchase is handled by Apple, which does not count as
collection by the developer. The game has no networking, analytics, crash reporting or ads in its code, on iPhone and
iPad it has no Game Center and sends no notifications, its iOS export has tracking switched off, and saves, settings
and the record of the purchase are files on the device that never leave it. The Google Play build's local reminders do
not exist in the iPhone and iPad build.
