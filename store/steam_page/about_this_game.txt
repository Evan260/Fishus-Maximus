[h2]Your uncle has left you his old boat. The bay is full of fish.[/h2]

Fishus Maximus is a cozy fishing game set on the Bay of Naples in AD 72, in the same harbour town as Mosaicus Maximus, with Vesuvius green and quiet behind it. Row out at dawn, watch the fish swim under your boat, and bring them in one by one. The Romans were mad about fish, and the catch is only the start: keep the finest alive in your own pond, fill the town's orders, and make garum from the rest.

[h2]A rod, a float and a fish that fights back[/h2]
[list]
[*]The view is cut through at the waterline, so you see every fish you are fishing for: what comes to look at your bait, what nibbles, and what finally takes it.
[*]Cast, wait for the float to go under, strike, then give line while the fish runs and reel while it rests. Big fish leap clear of the water.
[*]Every catch is hung out on a painted linen banner with its weight and grade, and a starfish when it is new to your logbook.
[*]Four ways to fish: the rod and line, wicker pots set on the seabed, a cast net over a shoal, and the trident at night by the light of the lamp.
[*]Gentle fishing in the settings keeps the line from snapping, for a calmer evening.
[/list]

[h2]Sixty kinds of fish, all ones the Romans knew[/h2]
Red mullet, gilt-head bream, moray eels, octopus, tuna, swordfish and many more, each with its own places, hours, seasons, weather and favourite bait. Your logbook keeps every fish you catch and your best weight for each. Twelve legends come from the old stories, from the giant octopus of Carteia to the mullet Tiberius sold at auction and a moray so old it answers to its name. The townsfolk drop the clues.

[h2]Six places along the bay[/h2]
The harbour, the rocky shore and its caves, the oyster beds of the Lucrine Lake, the open bay, the sea caves of Capri and the deep water off Cape Misenum. Each is a long stretch of coast drawn in many layers, from the sunrise to the stars, with rain, mist, storms and on rare clear nights the northern lights.

[h2]A pond of your own[/h2]
[list]
[*]Dig a fish pond into the rock by the beach on your estate and widen it as the chapters open.
[*]Give it rocks, caves for the morays, weed, sand, shade and a freshwater spring.
[*]Feed your fish, watch them grow and breed, and name your favourites. Ring the bell and the named ones swim to the front.
[*]Dress them up with finery from the goldsmith, earrings included, as the emperor's grandmother did for her moray.
[/list]

[h2]The harbour town[/h2]
[list]
[*]Sell your catch at the fish market, where a varied catch pays best.
[*]Fill orders from the harbour board, and from six regular clients with stories of their own, Trimalchio and his banquets among them.
[*]Restore your uncle's garum works tank by tank and make the sauce the Romans put on everything.
[*]Save for a bigger boat at the boatwright's: from the rowing skiff to the fishing boat, the sailing boat and the big boat with its lantern.
[/list]

[h2]Made to sit beside your work[/h2]
Let the fisherman fish on his own and shrink the game to a strip along the bottom of your screen. The boat bobs on its band of water and keeps fishing while you work, study or browse.

[h2]Little joys[/h2]
[list]
[*]Choose your dog's breed, coat and name. It rides in the boat, barks at a bite and wags when you pet it.
[*]Dolphins, the tuna run in spring, the Neptunalia fishing contest in July and the pond show.
[*]Calm harp music, lapping water, gulls by day and cicadas at dusk.
[*]42 Steam achievements, three saves, full controller support, and play in 14 languages.
[/list]

No timers and no failure. Just a quiet Roman bay and whatever is biting today.
